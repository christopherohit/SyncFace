\begin{abstract}
Neural Radiance Field (NeRF)-based talking head synthesis methods~\cite{guo2021adnerf,tang2022radnerf,li2023ernerf,peng2024synctalk} have achieved remarkable progress in generating photorealistic facial renderings from audio input.
However, a critical and underexplored limitation persists: \emph{temporal inconsistency}.
Because each frame is rendered independently with per-pixel photometric supervision alone, the resulting videos exhibit distracting flickering artifacts---sudden brightness shifts, jittering facial contours, and inconsistent texture details between consecutive frames---that severely degrade the perceived quality and realism of the generated videos.

In this work, we present a systematic investigation into the sources of temporal instability in NeRF-based talking head pipelines and propose \textbf{TemporalTalk}, a temporally regularized NeRF-based talking head framework that introduces three complementary, lightweight mechanisms to address temporal coherence without sacrificing per-frame rendering quality:
(1)~a \textbf{temporal consistency loss} that explicitly penalizes frame-to-frame prediction differences within facial regions, providing direct gradient signal for smooth transitions between consecutive frames;
(2)~a \textbf{multi-head self-attention mechanism} for audio temporal modeling that replaces fixed-kernel convolutional attention with content-dependent temporal weighting, enabling the network to adaptively focus on the most relevant audio context for each frame;
and (3)~a \textbf{cosine annealing learning rate schedule with linear warmup} that replaces exponential decay, providing more stable early training dynamics and better mid-training exploration.

Extensive experiments on the May dataset demonstrate that TemporalTalk reduces temporal flickering by 24\% (as measured by temporal LPIPS) while simultaneously improving per-frame synthesis quality across PSNR (+0.14~dB), LPIPS ($-$3.0\%), and landmark distance ($-$2.3\%).
Compared to prior methods including AD-NeRF~\cite{guo2021adnerf}, RAD-NeRF~\cite{tang2022radnerf}, ER-NeRF~\cite{li2023ernerf}, GeneFace~\cite{ye2023geneface}, and SyncTalk~\cite{peng2024synctalk}, our method achieves state-of-the-art results on all metrics.
Our ablation study confirms that each proposed component contributes meaningfully, with the temporal consistency loss providing the single largest gain in temporal stability.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Generating photorealistic talking head videos from audio input is a fundamental problem in computer vision and computer graphics, with wide-ranging applications in virtual avatars~\cite{thies2020nvp}, video conferencing, film production~\cite{kim2019dvp}, digital assistants, and accessible communication technologies.
Recent advances in Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF)~\cite{mildenhall2020nerf} have opened a promising avenue for this task: by conditioning the radiance field on audio features, methods such as AD-NeRF~\cite{guo2021adnerf}, RAD-NeRF~\cite{tang2022radnerf}, ER-NeRF~\cite{li2023ernerf}, GeneFace~\cite{ye2023geneface}, and SyncTalk~\cite{peng2024synctalk} can render individual frames of striking visual fidelity, often approaching photorealistic quality for single-image evaluation.
Meanwhile, 2D-based approaches including Wav2Lip~\cite{prajwal2020wav2lip}, SadTalker~\cite{zhang2023sadtalker}, MakeItTalk~\cite{zhou2020makelttalk}, and methods based on generative adversarial networks~\cite{karras2019stylegan,siarohin2019fomm} have demonstrated impressive results but typically lack the 3D consistency and view-dependent effects that NeRF naturally provides.

\paragraph{The Temporal Consistency Problem.}
Despite their impressive per-frame quality, all existing NeRF-based talking head methods suffer from a fundamental weakness: \emph{temporal inconsistency}.
When individual frames---each rendered independently---are assembled into a video sequence at typical frame rates (25--50 FPS), the result often exhibits noticeable and distracting flickering artifacts.
These artifacts manifest as brightness fluctuations, edge jittering along the jawline and lip boundaries, inconsistent texture details between adjacent frames, and subtle color shifts---all of which are immediately perceptible to the human visual system~\cite{wang2004ssim,zhang2018lpips}.

\paragraph{Root Cause Analysis.}
The fundamental cause of temporal flickering lies in the \emph{per-frame training paradigm} shared by all existing methods.
During training, each frame is optimized independently against its ground truth image, with no explicit constraint linking predictions across time.
Several factors compound this problem: (i) stochastic ray sampling introduces per-frame gradient variance; (ii) audio feature sensitivity causes small feature variations to produce disproportionate visual changes; (iii) hash grid quantization~\cite{mueller2022instant} introduces discontinuities near hash boundaries; and (iv) standard learning rate schedules (such as exponential decay) can create periods of training instability, particularly when hash grid features are near-random.

\paragraph{Our Approach: TemporalTalk.}
We propose \textbf{TemporalTalk}, a novel talking head synthesis framework built on tri-plane hash grid NeRF~\cite{mueller2022instant} that introduces three complementary mechanisms---operating at the output, input, and optimization levels, respectively---to systematically address temporal inconsistency:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Temporal Consistency Loss} (\S\ref{sec:temporal_loss}): An explicit loss term that penalizes L1 differences between predictions of consecutive frames in the face region, with warmup and masking strategies to avoid trivial solutions.

    \item \textbf{Temporal Audio Attention} (\S\ref{sec:temporal_attention}): A multi-head self-attention module~\cite{vaswani2017attention} over the audio feature sequence with sinusoidal positional encoding and a learnable query token, replacing fixed convolutional attention to enable content-dependent temporal weighting.

    \item \textbf{Cosine Learning Rate Schedule with Warmup} (\S\ref{sec:cosine_lr}): Cosine annealing~\cite{loshchilov2017sgdr} preceded by a linear warmup phase~\cite{goyal2017accurate} for stable early training and better mid-training exploration.
\end{enumerate}

Compared to six prior methods, TemporalTalk achieves state-of-the-art performance on all metrics, reducing temporal flickering by 24\% while improving per-frame quality.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\paragraph{Neural Radiance Fields for View Synthesis.}
Neural Radiance Fields (NeRF)~\cite{mildenhall2020nerf} represent a scene as a continuous volumetric function.
Since the original work, numerous extensions have improved rendering speed~\cite{mueller2022instant,fridovich2022plenoxels,chen2022tensorf}, quality~\cite{barron2021mipnerf,barron2022mipnerf360}, and generalizability~\cite{yu2021pixelnerf,wang2021ibrnet}.
Instant-NGP~\cite{mueller2022instant} introduced multi-resolution hash grid encoding that enables real-time applications.
More recently, 3D Gaussian Splatting~\cite{kerbl20233dgs} offers an alternative representation with fast rendering, and works have adapted it for talking heads~\cite{yu2024gaussiantalker,li2024talkinggaussian}.

\paragraph{Audio-Driven Talking Head Synthesis.}
Audio-driven talking head synthesis spans 2D and 3D approaches.
2D methods include Wav2Lip~\cite{prajwal2020wav2lip}, MakeItTalk~\cite{zhou2020makelttalk}, PC-AVS~\cite{zhou2021pcavs}, SadTalker~\cite{zhang2023sadtalker}, and First Order Motion Model~\cite{siarohin2019fomm}.
Mesh-based 3D approaches include VOCA~\cite{cudeiro2019voca}, MeshTalk~\cite{richard2021meshtalk}, FaceFormer~\cite{fan2022faceformer}, and CodeTalker~\cite{xing2023codetalker}.

In the NeRF domain, AD-NeRF~\cite{guo2021adnerf} first conditioned NeRF on DeepSpeech~\cite{hannun2014deepspeech} features.
NerFACE~\cite{gafni2021nerface} uses 3DMM expression parameters~\cite{blanz1999morphable}.
DFRF~\cite{shen2022dfrf} introduced few-shot adaptation.
RAD-NeRF~\cite{tang2022radnerf} achieved real-time rendering via audio-spatial decomposition.
ER-NeRF~\cite{li2023ernerf} introduced region-aware tri-plane hash representations.
GeneFace~\cite{ye2023geneface} and GeneFace++~\cite{ye2023genefacepp} use generative models for improved generalization.
LiveSpeechPortraits~\cite{lu2021lsp} achieves real-time reenactment.
SyncTalk~\cite{peng2024synctalk} focuses on cross-modal synchronization with face-parsing-guided training~\cite{yu2018bisenet}.
Real3D-Portrait~\cite{ye2024real3dportrait} extends to one-shot generation.
However, \emph{none of these methods explicitly addresses temporal consistency}---each frame is optimized independently, producing flickering artifacts in the resulting videos.

\paragraph{Temporal Consistency in Neural Rendering.}
For dynamic scenes, D-NeRF~\cite{pumarola2021dnerf}, Nerfies~\cite{park2021nerfies}, and HyperNeRF~\cite{park2021hypernerf} model scene dynamics.
Neural Scene Flow Fields~\cite{li2021nsff} and Neural Volumes~\cite{lombardi2019neural} provide alternative dynamic representations.
RegNeRF~\cite{regnerf2022} regularizes static NeRF training.
In 2D, Blind Video Temporal Consistency~\cite{lai2018blind} and DVP~\cite{kim2019dvp} use flow-based temporal regularization.
Face2Face~\cite{thies2016face2face} and Neural Voice Puppetry~\cite{thies2020nvp} address face reenactment.
Our temporal consistency loss operates directly on rendered predictions without requiring explicit flow estimation, making it well-suited for the NeRF ray-based rendering setting.

\paragraph{Attention and Learning Rate Scheduling.}
Transformers~\cite{vaswani2017attention} with self-attention have become dominant for sequential modeling in NLP~\cite{devlin2019bert}, vision~\cite{dosovitskiy2020vit}, and audio~\cite{baevski2020wav2vec}.
Cosine annealing~\cite{loshchilov2017sgdr} with warmup~\cite{goyal2017accurate} has been widely adopted for stable training~\cite{he2016resnet,loshchilov2019adamw,smith2019super}.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

We describe each of our three proposed mechanisms in detail.
TemporalTalk builds upon a tri-plane hash grid NeRF backbone~\cite{mueller2022instant} with audio-conditioned rendering, face-parsing-guided training~\cite{yu2018bisenet}, and blendshape-based eye control~\cite{deng2019accurate,feng2021deca}.
Our three contributions are modular and can be integrated into any NeRF-based talking head pipeline.

\subsection{Temporal Consistency Loss}
\label{sec:temporal_loss}

\paragraph{What it is.}
We introduce a temporal consistency loss that penalizes differences between predictions for consecutive frames within the facial region.
We store the previous frame's prediction $\hat{I}_{t-1}$ (detached from the computation graph) and compute the L1 distance with the current prediction $\hat{I}_t$:

\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\text{temporal}} = \frac{\lambda_{\text{temp}}}{|\mathcal{M}|} \sum_{p \in \mathcal{M}} \left\|\hat{I}_t(p) - \text{sg}\left[\hat{I}_{t-1}(p)\right]\right\|_1
    \label{eq:temporal_loss}
\end{equation}

where $\mathcal{M}$ is the face mask~\cite{yu2018bisenet}, $\lambda_{\text{temp}} = 0.1$, and $\text{sg}[\cdot]$ is the stop-gradient operator.
This formulation is inspired by temporal regularization in video processing~\cite{lai2018blind} but adapted for NeRF ray-based rendering without explicit optical flow.

\paragraph{Why this design.}
Four design choices are critical:
(1)~\textbf{Stop-gradient}: Detaching $\hat{I}_{t-1}$ prevents collapse to constant output---gradients flow only through $\hat{I}_t$, so the network must still match the ground truth.
(2)~\textbf{Face mask}: Restricting to face pixels focuses the constraint where flickering is most perceptible---mouth, eyes, and contours---without interfering with torso/background.
(3)~\textbf{Warmup delay}: The loss activates after $N_{\text{warm}} = 10{,}000$ steps to allow basic geometry learning first.
(4)~\textbf{L1 formulation}: L1 is more robust than L2 to legitimate rapid motions (blinks, plosives) that should not be suppressed.

The total training objective becomes:
$\mathcal{L}_{\text{total}} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{temporal}} + \mathcal{L}_{\text{reg}}$,
where $\mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}}$ is L1 + perceptual~\cite{zhang2018lpips} loss and $\mathcal{L}_{\text{reg}}$ includes ambient and uncertainty losses.
In ablation (\S\ref{sec:ablation}), temporal loss alone reduces tLPIPS by 18.2\%, the single largest improvement from any component.


\subsection{Temporal Audio Attention}
\label{sec:temporal_attention}

\paragraph{What it is.}
We replace convolutional audio attention (five stacked Conv1d layers $64 {\to} 16 {\to} 8 {\to} 4 {\to} 2 {\to} 1$ with softmax pooling) with a Transformer-style~\cite{vaswani2017attention} multi-head self-attention module:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Sinusoidal positional encoding}~\cite{vaswani2017attention} provides temporal position information.
    \item \textbf{Multi-head self-attention} (4 heads, $d{=}64$) with feed-forward network:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathbf{h} = \text{LN}(\mathbf{x} + \text{MultiHead}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}, \mathbf{x}))
    \end{equation}
    \begin{equation}
        \mathbf{o} = \text{LN}(\mathbf{h} + \text{FFN}(\mathbf{h}))
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Learnable query token} $\mathbf{q} \in \mathbb{R}^d$ pools the sequence via cross-attention:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathbf{a}_{\text{out}} = \text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{\mathbf{q} \cdot \mathbf{o}^\top}{\sqrt{d}}\right) \mathbf{o}
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Why self-attention.}
Convolutional attention applies fixed kernels regardless of content.
Self-attention computes \emph{content-dependent} weights, allowing adaptive temporal focus: narrow during sustained vowels, broader during consonant transitions, and context-seeking during silence.
This is analogous to how Transformers in FaceFormer~\cite{fan2022faceformer} and CodeTalker~\cite{xing2023codetalker} learn content-dependent speech-to-motion mappings.

\paragraph{Impact.}
Smoother audio feature trajectories translate to smoother visual output.
Adding temporal attention reduces tLPIPS by an additional 3.3\% beyond the temporal loss alone.


\subsection{Cosine Learning Rate Schedule with Warmup}
\label{sec:cosine_lr}

\paragraph{What it is.}
We use cosine annealing~\cite{loshchilov2017sgdr} with linear warmup~\cite{goyal2017accurate}:
\begin{equation}
    \text{lr}(t) = \begin{cases}
        \text{lr}_0 \cdot \dfrac{t}{T_w} & \text{if } t < T_w \\[6pt]
        \dfrac{\text{lr}_0}{2}\!\left(1 + \cos\!\left(\dfrac{\pi(t - T_w)}{T - T_w}\right)\right) & \text{if } t \geq T_w
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:cosine_lr}
\end{equation}
with $\text{lr}_0 = 10^{-3}$, $T_w = 2{,}000$ warmup steps, $T$ total steps.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Dataset.}
We evaluate on the \textbf{May} talking head dataset following the standard evaluation protocol~\cite{peng2024synctalk}.
The dataset consists of a monocular video preprocessed with 3DMM-based face tracking~\cite{blanz1999morphable,deng2019accurate}, face parsing~\cite{yu2018bisenet}, and audio feature extraction (AVE model).
Resolution is $450 \times 450$ at 50 FPS with ${\sim}$5,520 training frames.

\paragraph{Training.}
AdamW~\cite{loshchilov2019adamw}, $\beta{=}(0, 0.99)$, 65,536 rays/step, 100K iterations, FP16.
Hash grid: 12 levels, resolution 64--512~\cite{mueller2022instant}.
Temporal loss: $\lambda_{\text{temp}}{=}0.1$, warmup $10$K steps.
Cosine LR: $\text{lr}_0{=}10^{-3}$, warmup $T_w{=}2$K steps.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
PSNR ($\uparrow$), LPIPS~\cite{zhang2018lpips} ($\downarrow$, AlexNet~\cite{krizhevsky2012alexnet}), LMD ($\downarrow$, FAN~\cite{bulat2017fan}), and tLPIPS ($\downarrow$, temporal LPIPS between consecutive frames).

\paragraph{Compared methods.}
Wav2Lip~\cite{prajwal2020wav2lip}, AD-NeRF~\cite{guo2021adnerf}, GeneFace~\cite{ye2023geneface}, RAD-NeRF~\cite{tang2022radnerf}, ER-NeRF~\cite{li2023ernerf}, and SyncTalk~\cite{peng2024synctalk}.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Complementarity.}
Our three mechanisms operate at different pipeline levels: \emph{output} (temporal loss penalizes rendered frame differences), \emph{input} (self-attention produces smoother audio features), and \emph{optimization} (cosine LR stabilizes training dynamics).
Because they target different flickering sources, their improvements compound rather than overlap (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}).
This is a key insight: \emph{no single mechanism is sufficient}---temporal coherence requires addressing the problem at multiple levels simultaneously.

\paragraph{Why existing methods flicker.}
As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:method_comparison}, all prior methods~\cite{guo2021adnerf,ye2023geneface,tang2022radnerf,li2023ernerf,peng2024synctalk} share three limitations: (1) no temporal loss, (2) convolutional or no audio attention, and (3) exponential LR decay.
TemporalTalk is the first to address all three, explaining its superior temporal consistency.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
(1)~The temporal loss could over-smooth rapid movements; future work may use adaptive weighting based on audio energy~\cite{hsu2021hubert}.
(2)~Evaluation is on a single subject; multi-subject evaluation~\cite{peng2024synctalk,lu2021lsp} would strengthen conclusions.
(3)~Our framework is NeRF-specific; adapting to Gaussian Splatting~\cite{kerbl20233dgs,yu2024gaussiantalker,li2024talkinggaussian} is an open direction.
(4)~Minor memory overhead from storing one previous prediction, and $O(n^2)$ attention complexity for $n{=}8$ frames (negligible vs.\ rendering cost).

\paragraph{Broader impact.}
The principle of multi-level temporal regularization (output + input + optimization) is general and applicable to any per-frame neural rendering pipeline~\cite{pumarola2021dnerf,park2021nerfies,park2021hypernerf,li2021nsff,lombardi2019neural}.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented \textbf{TemporalTalk}, a temporally consistent talking head synthesis framework that introduces three complementary mechanisms: temporal consistency loss, multi-head self-attention for audio, and cosine LR with warmup.
These mechanisms operate at the output, input, and optimization levels respectively, achieving a 24.1\% reduction in temporal flickering while improving all per-frame metrics.
Compared to six prior methods including AD-NeRF~\cite{guo2021adnerf}, GeneFace~\cite{ye2023geneface}, ER-NeRF~\cite{li2023ernerf}, and SyncTalk~\cite{peng2024synctalk}, TemporalTalk achieves state-of-the-art results across PSNR, LPIPS~\cite{zhang2018lpips}, LMD, and tLPIPS.
Our work demonstrates that explicit temporal supervision at multiple levels is both necessary and effective for NeRF-based talking head synthesis, providing a practical framework with no inference-time overhead.
